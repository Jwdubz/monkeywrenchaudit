What your site is costing you — Monkey Wrench Plumbing —

I'm Jarrett, a Las Vegas web designer. I went through your site the way a homeowner with a leak would: find you, check you out, call. It is one of the more complete local plumbing sites I've looked at: one phone number everywhere, your license number, 601 reviews, a page for every service and a page for each service area. The leaks are small and mostly technical: slow loading on a phone, a script from OpenAI that sets cookies your privacy policy doesn't mention, a robots.txt line that blocks your site's own files from crawlers, and 21 service-area pages that largely repeat each other.

Checked October 17, 2026

What I found

01

Your pages are slow to show their main content on a phone.

In a simulated slow-connection load test on a phone-size screen, the main content appeared at 7.7 seconds on the homepage, 7.3 on Services, 7.2 on Contact, 7.1 on Free Quote and 6.7 on the Blog. Google calls 2.5 seconds good. Your Water Heaters page showed its main content at 2.8 seconds. The homepage transferred 4.1 MB, and 3.3 MB of that is the hero.mp4 video. Speed scores were 53 and 64 out of 100 on the homepage (two runs), 62 on Services, 68 on Blog, 69 on Contact, 73 on Free Quote and 87 on Water Heaters. This is one test setup, six pages. Field speed is unmeasured.

02

A script from OpenAI sets cookies on every page, and your privacy policy doesn't mention cookies.

On all six pages I tested, Lighthouse found four third-party cookies set by bzrcdn.openai.com and bzr.openai.com, which is why best practices scored 79 on each. Your site loads Google Tag Manager (GTM-PBLXWJXQ), and I can't see inside it, so I can't say who added that script or whether it was on purpose. Your Privacy Policy is 683 words and doesn't use the words cookies, analytics, pixel, advertising or tracking.

03

Your robots.txt tells every crawler to stay out of /_next/.

The file says Disallow: /_next/. That folder holds your site's scripts and styles, and your photos load through it (/_next/image). Google asks sites not to block those files, because it uses them to see a page the way a visitor does. Your page text is in the raw HTML (about 1,859 words on the homepage), so I can't say how much this costs you.

04

Your 21 service-area pages share most of their wording.

I compared the text of the 21 pages under /service-areas/, such as Anthem, Blue Diamond and Boulder City, by counting six-word phrases they have in common. A typical pair shares 64% of its phrases (the range is 56% to 82%), and they run about 880 words each. Your 19 service pages share about 30%. I can't say how Google treats this. It does mean a large part of each area page repeats the others.

05

For the services you sell, your pages mostly weren't among the search results.

I ran four searches that don't contain your name. Your site appeared in one: 24/7 emergency plumber, as the fifth result. It did not appear for "plumber Las Vegas", tankless water heater installation or water softener installation, even though you have a page for each of the last two. Active Plumbing was named in all four searches, and Roto-Rooter, CG Plumbing Solutions, Drip Doctors and others in some. A search for your name found you.

06

Two small accessibility slips.

On the Free Quote page the numbers 1, 2 and 3 are orange (#ffa63a) on white, a contrast ratio of 1.95 where 4.5 is expected for text that size. On the homepage the "Mention code MW100 when you book" heading is a third-level heading with no second-level heading before it. Accessibility scored 96 to 100 on every page.

What's working

Every page I opened has its own title and description, a canonical address and one page heading. Your phone number, (702) 900-5992, is the same in the text, the tap-to-call link and your business markup. Your NSCB license number, BBB accreditation, hours and address are on the site, your markup lists your BBB, Facebook, Instagram, Yelp and YouTube profiles, and the pages say 5.0 from 601 Google reviews. The pages don't depend on scripts to show their text. You have an llms.txt file, a sitemap of 164 pages and a page for each of 19 services. SEO scored 100 on every page I tested.

Unmeasured

Visitor numbers, rankings, how the site looks on a phone (untested), whether the contact form reaches you, your live Google review count and what your Google listing shows. I didn't submit any forms or look at private data.

What I checked on your site

  • Found
    Not blocking AI crawlers by name

    Your robots.txt lists your sitemap and doesn't name any crawler. I tested 11 well-known AI and search crawler names (GPTBot, ChatGPT-User, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended, CCBot, Bytespider, Amazonbot) and none is blocked by name.

  • Found
    Page text is readable without scripts

    The homepage HTML contains about 1,859 words and one clear heading (“24/7 Licensed Las Vegas Plumber”), so a crawler that doesn't run JavaScript can still read it.

  • Found
    Business markup

    The homepage carries structured data for a Plumber: name, address, phone, hours, price range, location, rating and profile links. It also has a set of FAQ questions with answers.

  • Found
    An llms.txt file

    /llms.txt exists and gives your name, license, address, phone, hours and service radius, plus when to recommend you. It is a newer, optional file that some AI tools read.

  • Found
    Title and description on every page

    All eleven pages I opened have their own title (47 to 60 characters) and description.

  • Found
    Phone is a tap-to-call link

    Every page I checked uses (702) 900-5992, the same number as in your markup.

  • Gap
    robots.txt blocks /_next/

    Disallow: /_next/ applies to every crawler. That folder holds the scripts, styles and resized photos your pages use, and Google asks sites not to block them.

  • Note
    Review count

    A written search answer called your 5.0 rating “552 Google reviews”, while your pages now say 601. I couldn't check the live Google count, so I can't say which is current. It shows that search answers can lag behind your page.

These are plain checks anyone can repeat on October 17, 2026: what the site publishes, not a score.
